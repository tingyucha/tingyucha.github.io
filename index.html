I am a Scientist at the NSF National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCAR), where I bridge the gap between observational data and numerical weather prediction. I leverage polarimetric radar, large satellite datasets, and modeling frameworks to investigate tropical convection, hurricane dynamics, and rainfall intensity. After earning my Ph.D. in Atmospheric Science at Colorado State University, I joined NCAR as an Advanced Study Program (ASP) Postdoctoral Fellow before transitioning to my current role. I am also dedicated to community tools, contributing to the LROSE project and serving as an Associate Editor for the Journal of Atmospheric and Oceanic Technology. Outside of work, you can usually find me hiking, snowboarding, dirt biking, or traveling.
